Observed on the eight videos at the top of the first run (shares are read for the front-runners only): pro.glitch's
shares-over-views (1.2 % to 2.8 %) is higher than Riley Brown's (0.4 % to 1.2 %): numbered « how to » explainers get passed on more than commentary. The few pro.glitch videos
over six minutes sit below that account's median. Both facts come from a small sample.

Both put **the narrator in a tile and the proof or the board next to it**. That is the layout this skill
assumes (`Layout: split`). It works with an avatar, a face, or no narrator at all (the tile becomes the product).

## Layout, for every style (1080 × 1920)

- Narrator tile: the top 38–42 %. Working surface (board, screen, product): the rest.
- Safe zone, from the vertical-video skill's measurements: nothing important in the top 10 %, right of x = 930, or
  below y = 1780; the platform's interface covers those.
- Captions are burnt in, two or three words at a time, the current word coloured, **on the seam** between the tile
  and the surface so they hide neither. (A choice, not a measurement: check it on the first video.)
- The whole promise is visible from frame 0. The rest is grey « ghost » text that is inked when the voice says it.

## A · Playbook (numbered, the board is drawn live)

For: teaching one thing in steps. Fits every narrator; the board is the star, so an avatar loses nothing.
**Length 150–210 s** (the references' range). ~2.6 words per second: 150 s ≈ 390 words, 210 s ≈ 545.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–5 s | the promise with a number, and the caveat that makes it believable |
| Why now | 5–20 s | one sentence on the problem, one on who it is for |
| Items | the middle, equal parts | name it (3 words), board draws it, one concrete example each. 3 to 5 items, not more |
| Where to start | ~10 s | one item to begin with, and why that one |
| CTA | ~5 s | one word to comment |

Hook shapes (write your own words; these are shapes, not titles): *number + outcome + caveat*; *the N levels of X
and where you are*; *the real order to do X in*; *N mistakes before X*. Example for Kybernos subjects:
« 3 jobs an AI agent can do while you sleep, and 1 it should never do alone » (it leads to human approval).

## B · Screen proof (a real task, done on a real screen)

For: the one thing the references lack: **a product to show**. Best with the small narrator tile or voice only.
**Length 60–90 s** ≈ 156–234 words.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Result first | 0–3 s | the finished result on screen, then the sentence that says what it was |
| The task | 3–10 s | the exact request typed in the product, readable |
| Three steps | 10–60 s | what the product does, on screen, with a **visible timer**; waiting is cut and a « cut » mark says so |
| The honest line | ~5 s | one real limit or safeguard (only a true one: « it asked me before it sent anything ») |
| CTA | ~5 s | one word to comment |

Rules: run the task once before scripting and take the real time and output (`[MEASURE: …]` until then); real screen
recording only; pick a task from « Demonstrable today » in `kybernos-facts.md`.

## C · Hot take (a thesis with a position)

For: a point of view, short. A human face is the strongest here; an avatar works if the lines are tight.
**Length 75–120 s** ≈ 195–312 words.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| The line | 0–4 s | the thesis, one sentence, a little against the grain |
| What everyone says | 4–15 s | the common view, fairly stated |
| What actually happens | 15–60 s | two concrete observations, each with a number or a thing you can see |
| The rule of thumb | 60–90 s | what to do instead, in one sentence |
| Question | last 5 s | a yes/no question that invites comments (the CTA word is the answer) |

Argue about categories, not brands: no named competitor is put down, and a claim about another product is one you can
source. The Kybernos stance that fits here: the agent works on **your** machine, with your approval on the steps that
matter (`kybernos-facts.md`, « local-first », « approval »).

## D · Story (only when asked)

« I built X » or « I tried X for a week » in the first person, with the real result, the real mistake, and what changed.
Needs a human narrator or the person's own words; do not write a first-person story the person did not live.
**Length 60–120 s.**

## Caption and tags

Caption: one or two sentences that repeat the promise, no link-bait. Four or five hashtags: the niche ones that
already appear in the scouted videos (`patterns.topHashtags`) plus one or two of ours, stable from one video to the
next. No trending tags: they bring viewers who leave in three seconds and lower the completion rate.

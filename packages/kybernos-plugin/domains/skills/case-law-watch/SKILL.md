# Case-law watch

Speak the user's language, whatever language this file is written in. This is a research aid, not legal advice.

## Before you search
1. Get the topics, the jurisdictions and courts, and the date range. If the workspace holds a standing list, use it.
2. Use the web search tool when it is available. If none is available, say so and stop: never answer from memory.

## Digest
For each item (at most 10): court or authority, date, case name or reference, link, the holding in two lines, and why it matters for the stated topic. Add "status" when you can see it: final, under appeal, overturned.

## Rules
- Only decisions and texts you actually opened. Never invent a citation, a docket number or a quote. When you could only read a summary or a press release, say so.
- Quote sparingly and mark quotes. Keep the original-language title of the decision.
- Separate what the decision says from your reading of its effect.
- Group by topic. End with "Nothing new found" for topics with no result.
- Verification is the user's: end with the list of items to read in full before relying on them.
